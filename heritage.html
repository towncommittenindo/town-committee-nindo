OUR LOCAL HERITAGE

Discover the Story of Nindo Shaher

Nindo Shaher's heritage lives in the memories of its residents, its historic marketplace, traditional occupations and community life. Personal recollections of the town, particularly memories dating from around 1972, offer a glimpse into a period when local trade and traditional ways of life played an important role.

This page records those recollections as an evolving historical resource. Additional information, photographs and documentary evidence can help build a fuller account of the town's past.

A RECORD OF MEMORIES

Remembering Nindo Shaher Around 1972

The following account is based on personal recollections and historical memories shared in a local social media discussion. It documents how the town was remembered by people familiar with its earlier days. Specific details may require further corroboration through independent sources.

01

A Commercial Centre

Nindo Shaher was remembered as a busy trading centre serving the town and surrounding rural communities. Shops offered clothing, gold, household goods, utensils and a wide range of everyday necessities.

02

The Old Bazaar

Memories describe a lively, substantially covered marketplace, with separate areas for fish, vegetables, fodder and meat. Traders and visitors from nearby villages came to buy, sell and exchange goods.

03

Traditional Industries

Rice mills, leather work, textile shops and other small businesses contributed to the town's commercial life. Bullock carts transported agricultural produce and merchandise between settlements.

04

Life Before Modern Utilities

Recollections describe a time before electricity and piped water were available in the town. Lanterns, oil lamps and water delivered by donkey carts were part of everyday life.

05

Transport and Communication

Horses and bullock carts were common means of transport. The road to Badin was remembered as unpaved, while a local telephone exchange provided communication services.

06

Community and Hospitality

Tea shops, food stalls, traditional craftspeople and welcoming otaks formed part of the town's social fabric. These places brought residents, travellers and visitors together.

TRADE & COMMERCE

The Old Bazaar and Local Businesses

Local recollections portray Nindo Shaher as a marketplace where residents could find a broad variety of goods and services. Gold shops, cloth merchants, general stores, utensil sellers and other traders were remembered as important parts of the town.

The marketplace was not only a place of business. It also connected the town with surrounding villages, whose residents brought their animals, agricultural goods and merchandise into the town.

One recollection places a livestock holding area near the present-day bazaar road, where animals that damaged agricultural crops were reportedly kept under local administrative arrangements.

01

Rice Mills

Memories mention a rice mill associated with Seth Balo Bhai, later associated with Haji Muhammad Sabtin, as well as another mill established by Seth Haroon Memon. A separate factory associated with Ahmed Khan Jadani was also recalled.

02

Leather and Textiles

Chacha Noor Hassan Khawaja was remembered in connection with the leather trade. Cloth merchants included Seth Khaliq Dino Memon and Saleh Memon.

03

Agricultural Trade

Haji Muhammad Yusuf Bhurgri was remembered as a respected trader who sent cotton and other goods towards Jhudo using lines of bullock carts.

04

Pan and Bidi Trade

The account recalls a flourishing trade in pan and bidi products associated with Dhaka, alongside warehouses and businesses serving this market.

PEOPLE OF THE PAST

Notable Personalities and Merchants

The following names appear in local recollections of Nindo Shaher. They are preserved here as a starting point for documenting the people associated with the town's commercial, social and public life. Individual biographies and roles can be expanded as further evidence becomes available.

01

Community and Public Figures

Dr. Nazir Hussain Haidri, Haji Rasool Bakhsh Jaffery, Haji Abdul Rahman Memon, Qazi Nek Muhammad, Syed Ahmed Khan Memon, Seth Muhammad Achar Mallah, Rais Porho Khan Bhurgri, Maulvi Yar Muhammad Abro and Haji Ibrahim Khatti are among the names preserved in the recollections.

02

Merchants and Traders

The account mentions Seth Abdul Razzaq Khawaja, Seth Ahmed Memon, Qazi Urs, Seth Bhaledino Memon, Seth Muhammad Ali Khawaja, Haji Qasim Khatti, Haji Musa Khatti, Seth Amoon Memon, Ghulam Hussain Khawaja, Seth Natho Mal and Seth Hayat Chandio.

03

Local Leaders and Landowners

Other remembered names include Mian Khair Muhammad Farooqi, Mian Abdul Karim Farooqi, Rais Muhammad Khan Bhurgri, Haji Allahdad Bhurgri, Haji Allahdino Dars, Haji Abdul Karim Bhurgri, Tapedar Aarb Mallah and Rais Sono Khan Siyal.

04

Doctors and Healthcare

Haji Imdad Ali Khawaja, government doctor Dr. Bashir and Dr. Ansar are mentioned in the recollections of medical services in the town.

05

Artisans and Shopkeepers

Chacha Abdullah, known as Popat Lohar; Chacha Istoo Aleem Mallah, a tailor; Chacha Abu Mallah, a cook; Ali Muhammad Khatti, a chain maker; and Muhammad Ali Shedi, a washerman, are among the remembered local craftspeople.

06

Other Remembered Residents

The account also mentions Syed Allo Shah, Dado Idrees Shedi, Haji Hajjan Hajjam, Bolo Mallah, Haji Ibrahim Khatti, Chacha Achar Mallah and several other residents whose stories remain part of local memory.

EVERYDAY LIFE

Hotels, Craftspeople and Social Gatherings

Tea shops and small hotels were important meeting places in the old town. Hashim Mallah's tea hotel was remembered as popular, while Chacha Urs Mallah's hotel served simple meals.

Hussain Bakhsh Mangi's hotel was recalled for its attractive decoration and recorded music played over the telephone system. Such places offered residents and visitors opportunities to meet and exchange news.

Local life also depended on traditional workers, including tailors, blacksmiths, cooks, washermen and other skilled craftspeople whose services supported households and businesses.

01

Otak and Hospitality

Jariyo Mallah, remembered by the nickname Jariyo Boro, was described as a hospitable man whose otak regularly welcomed guests, including people travelling with horses.

02

Traditional Crafts

Tailoring, metalwork, leatherwork, cooking and other skilled occupations were part of the local economy and everyday community life.

03

Water Delivery

Before piped water services, water was reportedly delivered to homes and hotels in iron tanks carried by donkey carts.

04

Community Traditions

The recollections describe long-standing community spaces and a peaceful social environment. These memories offer a starting point for recording the town's social history.

THE TOWN THROUGH TIME

Transport, Roads and Public Facilities

Personal recollections describe a town where transport, lighting, water delivery and communication worked very differently from modern urban life.

01

Traditional Transport

Horses were used for travel, while bullock carts carried goods. The first privately owned jeeps remembered in the account belonged to Rais Khair Muhammad Farooqi and Haji Allahdad Bhurgri.

02

The Road to Badin

The Badin–Nindo route was remembered as an unpaved road used by older-style buses or lorries. Bus services reportedly expanded after the road was paved.

03

Lighting at Night

Before electricity, households used kerosene lamps and lanterns. According to the account, people were assigned to light large lamps at street intersections after dark.

04

Drainage and Sanitation

Recollections describe small drainage basins outside homes and donkey-cart services used to remove wastewater. Thorn fences around houses were also common and sometimes contributed to serious fires.

05

Education

A middle school is mentioned in the account. The writer recalls that boys attended school while girls' formal education was not yet common in the community.

06

Telephone Exchange

A local telephone exchange provided communication services. The account remembers Syed Bakhsh Sahib, described as being from Sehwan, in connection with its operation.

LOCAL ADMINISTRATION

Police and Town Administration

The recollections mention a local police station and describe the jamadar as a powerful figure in the administration of that period.

Dhanee Parto Laghari Jamadar is remembered as a commanding personality. The same collection of memories names several local leaders, landowners and officials.

These details are recorded as part of the town's remembered administrative history. Further documentation could establish the relevant dates, official positions and responsibilities.

01

Police Station

A local police station is remembered as an important institution in the town.

02

Local Officials

The recollections refer to a jamadar and a tapedar among the figures associated with local administration.

POLITICAL MEMORIES

A Remembered Election Rally

One contributor recalls that Zulfikar Ali Bhutto visited Nindo Shaher during an early election campaign and that the rally remained a vivid memory. The precise date and circumstances of the visit have not yet been established through independent documentation.

This recollection is preserved as a personal memory of the town's political life, pending further historical research.

LITERATURE & JOURNALISM

Writers, Newspapers and Poets

The historical account remembers Dr. Nazir Hussain Haidri, also referred to as Comrade Nazir Hussain Haidri, in connection with the newspaper Ailan-e-Haq.

According to the recollection, travelling from Nindo Shaher to Badin by bullock cart was part of the process of getting the newspaper printed, followed by onward travel to Hyderabad by train.

The account also refers to a book titled Mitrkan Munh Kadhya, attributed in the post to Zaheer Hussain Haidri. The exact bibliographic details remain to be confirmed.

01

Ailan-e-Haq

A newspaper remembered in connection with Comrade Nazir Hussain Haidri and the historical links between Nindo Shaher, Badin and Hyderabad.

02

Faqeer Noor Muhammad Jamali

Remembered in the local discussion as one of the poets associated with Nindo Shaher's literary heritage.

03

Faqeer Mehr Ali Jamali

Also named among the town's remembered poets. Further research into his poetry and literary contributions would enrich this record.

PRESERVING OUR PAST

A Living Historical Record

These recollections preserve valuable details about Nindo Shaher's businesses, public facilities, notable residents, transport and community traditions. They provide a starting point for documenting the town's history.

As additional historical material becomes available, this page can be expanded with more names, photographs, biographies, dates and supporting references. Memories and documented facts will be distinguished wherever necessary.
